Seven learning objectives that fit most health topics. Each comes with short Google Skills courses to set as optional preparation, a class activity, ways for students to show the outcome, and text you can copy into FLO. Prepared by A/Prof Matt Sutton, CHE, Flinders University, with assistance from Claude (Anthropic). Courses checked against the Google Skills catalogue on 6 and 7 October 2026.

How to use this

  1. Pick one objectiveChoose the one that fits a week or task you already teach. One is plenty for a topic.
  2. Adapt the wordingReplace [your discipline] and change anything that doesn't fit your topic or year level. Check with your Course Coordinator before adding a new learning outcome.
  3. Set the course as preparationCopy the FLO text into your topic, then use class time for the activity. The judging happens in class, not in the course.

Before you set it

  1. Keep it optional and ungradedA badge shows that a student finished a course and recalled its content. It can't show they met a learning outcome, so collect the evidence in your own class activity.
  2. Offer another routeStudents sign in with a personal Google Account and a Developer Profile. Give anyone who would rather not a short reading or class handout that covers the same idea.
  3. Use approved tools in classThe courses demonstrate Gemini, which Flinders hasn't approved yet. When students try anything themselves, they use Copilot signed in with their Flinders account.
  4. Don't make AI use compulsoryOutside guided first-year activities, a reasoned decision not to use a tool should be an acceptable answer.
