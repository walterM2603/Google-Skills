How Google Skills is organised

Google Skills is Google's training catalogue, formerly called Google Cloud Skills Boost. Everything sits in one catalogue. Learning paths bundle courses and labs into a sequence, and finishing things earns badges. Flinders staff reach it through the Flinders University organisation on the platform, which also has a Flinders AI Champions path of nine recommended courses.

What's in it

Most of the catalogue trains cloud engineers and developers. The part that matters for teaching staff is smaller: how generative AI works, checking outputs, responsible use, agents and a little on health. Bars show courses and paths in each subject group, to scale, as read on 7 October 2026.

AI Boost Bites74

Ten-minute videos with a one-question check, each on a single task such as summarising a document or reading a chart. The easiest place to start.

Short courses231

Courses of an hour or less. Concept courses on generative AI, agents and responsible use sit here.

Longer courses324

Over an hour, often several. Mostly technical, with a few that suit staff who lead AI work.

Learning paths78

A set sequence of courses. Useful when you want a structured route rather than single topics.

Hands-on labs705

Timed exercises in a temporary Google Cloud project. Almost all are for technical roles, and each uses lab credits.

Getting started

  1. Get accessFlinders staff access comes through the Flinders University organisation on Google Skills. You will also need a Google Account and a Google Developer Profile.
  2. Start smallPick one task group above and do its "Start here" course. Most take 10 to 45 minutes.
  3. Practise in CopilotThe courses demonstrate Gemini, which Flinders has not approved yet. Practise the ideas in Copilot, signed in with your Flinders login.
  4. Keep student work outDon't put student submissions into any AI tool for graded marking. Check the What's allowed page on the ETAIT site before using AI with student work.
  5. Setting it for studentsKeep it optional and ungraded, run the judging in class, and offer another route for students who would rather not create a Google account.
